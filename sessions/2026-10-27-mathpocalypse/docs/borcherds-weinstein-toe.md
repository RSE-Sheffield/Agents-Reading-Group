# Research note: Borcherds and Weinstein on *Theories of Everything*

**Source:** [Fields Medalist & Eric Weinstein on the Future of Mathematics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4),
*Theories of Everything with Curt Jaimungal*, published 9 November 2026 (about 2 hours). Richard
Borcherds (Fields Medal 1998, UC Berkeley) and Eric Weinstein (PhD in mathematics, Harvard) discuss
three things: what is at stake for mathematics, who gets credit now, and what a spinor is. Only the
opening, AI-related part is covered here.

## 1. The leap in capability

- **"Math is being entirely wiped out by AI."** ✓ This line is in the teaser at the very start
  ([00:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=1)) and appears to be Borcherds. He adds
  that this "was a real shock to pretty much all mathematicians".
- **From garbage to the frontier in months.** ✓ Borcherds:
  > "A couple of years ago, AI doing mathematics was kind of amusing — it would write
  > plausible-looking stuff that was, in fact, complete garbage when you looked at it closely. And
  > then suddenly, around summer this year, they went from being mediocre undergraduates to being
  > the best mathematicians on the planet."
  >
  > [01:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=109)
- **Redundancy.** ✓
  - Weinstein, probably: "We have to prepare for being made redundant in absolutely everything where
    we become a drag" ([00:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=20)).
  - Borcherds, probably: "AI is already better than me at everything. It's better at research, it's
    better at teaching. There's no reason for employing me as far as I can figure it out"
    ([01:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=61)).

## 2. What the AI is doing: the "convex hull" and low-hanging fruit

These quotes are unchecked (see the caveat above).

- **Weinstein's heuristic.** He describes the AI as "exploring the convex hull of things that we know
  how to do, but that have been too expensive for us to carry out". On this view it is "clearing
  the orchard of low-hanging fruit" ([05:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=308)).
- **Borcherds agrees.**
  > "...it's sort of going around putting things together that humans could have done, but there
  > were just so many things to try … too much effort for humans to work through these million
  > possibilities."
  >
  > [10:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=651)

## 3. Theory building as the human moat

- ✓ Weinstein: "But the AI is as yet nowhere in what I would call theory building"
  ([00:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=26)).
- Unchecked: he splits pure mathematics into **conjecture, problem-solving and theory building**.
  He says AI's progress across the three is very uneven, and that he is not claiming it will never
  build theories: "it's a matter of time" ([05:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=308)).

## 4. What's at stake

- **Students and careers.** Traditional training rests on mastering the literature and doing
  technical calculations. AI now does both instantly and in parallel. This raises questions about
  what graduate students should be trained for
  ([00:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=20)).
- **The "gamification" trap.** Unchecked. The speakers warn against treating maths as a list of
  open conjectures to tick off. Metrics of this kind miss the structural and conceptual side of the
  subject ([03:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqgnJyQZGG4&t=227)).

## Relevance to the session

- **The strongest "dread" quotes yet, from a Fields Medallist.** "Wiped out" and "no reason for
  employing me" are the Mathpocalypse in its purest form, which makes them good openers. The more
  measured content follows within minutes:
  - The AI is filling in the convex hull of what is already known.
  - Theory building is still human, for now.

  Present the exaggeration with a smile, as the session's framing does. Pair it with the optimistic
  readings: [3Blue1Brown](3blue1brown-better-time-for-math.md) on theory building as the creative
  core, and [Tao's Math 2.0](tao-math-2-0.md).
- **The "convex hull" picture is a good one for discussion.** It fits Gowers's
  [jagged frontier](jagged-frontier-gowers.md), where AI wins on breadth and volume. It also fits
  the "Sarnak test" and "LLMs can't jump" views on new abstractions. And it echoes Szegedy's
  "[satellites mapping the terrain](szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics.md)".
  - **Ask the group:** is the hull itself getting bigger with each model generation?
  - **Ask the group:** can a fast enough search of the hull look the same as a jump outside it?
- **Conjecture / problem-solving / theory building** is a three-way split that maps onto the
  "What do mathematicians actually do?" slide. Gamification matches Tao's point that solving
  problems was only ever a *proxy* goal.
