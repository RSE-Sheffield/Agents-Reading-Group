# Research community and institutional responses to AI-generated mathematics, 19 Oct – 19 Nov 2026

Method note: about 25 searches and fetches (19 Nov 2026). The densest sources were the community aggregator Proofs and Prompts newsfeed (https://proofsandprompts.com/newsfeed/), Terence Tao's blog, and the AGMAI website. I fetched the primary pages where possible. Status labels: **[confirmed]** means I read the primary source; **[reported]** means it comes from a secondary or aggregator source and I did not fetch the primary page; **[disputed]** means sources conflict.

## Q1. Mathematical societies, advisory bodies, journals and arXiv

### Takeaway
None of the big learned societies (IMU, AMS, LMS, EMS) put out a new formal statement in the window that I could find. The real institutional action came from (a) the IAS-hosted Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI (AGMAI), which issued two new recommendations to AI labs on 25–26 Oct; (b) six probability and statistics journals, which paused new submissions on 19 Oct because of an AI-driven surge in submissions; and (c) smaller publishers updating their AI policies (IMS, MSP). arXiv has said nothing specific about bulk AI releases. Its 1 Oct rate limit remains the relevant policy.

### Cited Findings
**AGMAI (IAS-hosted advisory group: Charles, De Lellis, Gowers, Hairer, Srivastava, Tillmann, Vakil, Witten, Wood)**
- [confirmed] 25 Oct 2026, "Disadvantages of Designating Mathematicians for AI Output Releases". The group recommends that AI labs stop sending AI output to selected mathematicians early. It cites delayed or unequal access, denied agency, biased selection and "borrowed reputation", and points labs to its release pathways 2A/2B instead. — [AGMAI](https://agmai.org/)
- [confirmed] 26 Oct 2026, "Recommendation for Immediate Release of Yang–Mills AI Output". The group asks OpenAI to publish now the natural-language and Lean outputs behind its announced Yang–Mills, QCD and Standard Model existence proofs. It says OpenAI's stated reason for waiting (finishing the Lean formalisation) no longer applies. — [AGMAI](https://agmai.org/)
- [confirmed] Context, outside the window: AGMAI's 6 Oct statement on OpenAI's release said "This release is the beginning, not the completion" of human understanding, and that its role was not an endorsement of OpenAI's process. — [AGMAI](https://agmai.org/)
- [reported] OpenAI announced the further advances on 26/27 Oct (local smoothing conjecture, refutation of ETH for 3SAT, Yang–Mills). By one account, local smoothing was released "without external collaboration". — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/), via [Proofs and Prompts newsfeed](https://proofsandprompts.com/newsfeed/)
- [reported] 11 Nov 2026: a validation team (David Gross, Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov, Nikita Nekrasov) validated OpenAI's Yang–Mills claim and released the initial submission. Scott Armstrong and Martin Hairer each started public "humanisation" repositories. — [assessment](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/YM/blob/main/docs/assessment-1.pdf); [Armstrong](https://github.com/scottnarmstrong/yang-mills-informalization); [Hairer](https://github.com/hairer/humanise-yang-mills) (via the newsfeed)
- [reported] 16 Nov 2026: theoretical high-energy physicists published an open letter calling for open release of AI-produced results, partly prompted by the Yang–Mills handling. — [hep-open-release.org](https://hep-open-release.org) (via the newsfeed)

**Journals and publishers**
- [confirmed] On 19 Oct 2026, EDP Sciences announced that ESAIM: Probability and Statistics would take no new submissions from 19 Oct to 8 Nov, reopening 9 Nov. The publisher cited "a substantial increase in manuscript submissions across the discipline… driven largely by the rapid development of artificial intelligence". Revisions and reviews already under way continue. The board will use the pause to review scope and its AI guidance for authors and reviewers. — [EDP Sciences](https://www.edpsciences.org/en/news-highlights/3058-esaim-probability-and-statistics-takes-action-to-address-increasing-manuscript-submissions); [MACCUS, 26 Oct](https://maccus.fr/en/ai-paper-flood/)
- [reported] It was a coordinated pause by six journals: ESAIM: P&S, Electronic Journal of Probability, Electronic Communications in Probability, Stochastic Processes and their Applications, Bernoulli, and Annales de l'IHP Probabilités et Statistiques. Exact dates are confirmed only for ESAIM. — [MACCUS](https://maccus.fr/en/ai-paper-flood/)
- [reported] The Institute of Mathematical Statistics updated its AI policy on 8 Nov 2026. — [IMS policy](https://imstat.org/journals-and-publications/policy-on-use-of-ai/) (via the newsfeed)
- [reported] MSP (the nonprofit publisher of Geometry & Topology, ANT and others) released a "deliberately minimal" draft AI policy on 1 Nov 2026. — [MSP](https://msp.org/publications/ai/) (via the newsfeed)
- No editorial or policy found from Annals, Acta, JAMS or Inventiones in the window.

**arXiv**
- [confirmed] The arXiv blog has nothing on AI or bulk releases in the window. The posts from 19 Oct to 19 Nov cover database maintenance (21 Oct) and a holiday schedule (6 Nov). The relevant policy is still the 1 Oct "Fair Moderation, Equitable Access, and AI" rate limit. — [arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/); [1 Oct post](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/10/01/updated-rate-limit-policy/)
- [reported] That rate limit allows two submissions per author per calendar month and no more than three active at once. arXiv cited a record 40,363 submissions in Sep 2026. — [Research Information](https://www.researchinformation.info/news/arxiv-imposes-submission-limits-amid-surge-in-ai-generated-papers/); [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/arxiv-is-rate-limiting-submissions-because-it-cant-keep-up-with-ai-slop/)

### Inferences
- In this window the institutional response shifted from statements of principle (Leiden, Fields letter, AHM) to practical measures for coping with volume: journal pauses, publisher AI policies, arXiv throttling. Release norms were handled by AGMAI rather than by the societies.
- AGMAI's two new recommendations implicitly criticise OpenAI's practice of releasing to chosen mathematicians first and of delaying Yang–Mills. The group OpenAI cited as consulted is now publicly pushing back on how it does things.

### Gaps
- No window statements found from IMU, AMS, LMS, EMS or the IAS itself (as distinct from AGMAI). I did not search the society websites page by page, so a minor item could have been missed.
- The Academy for the Mathematical Sciences report content was not verified (403).
- I did not confirm the pause dates for the five non-ESAIM probability journals, or whether they reopened on 9 Nov.

## Q2. Funders (UKRI/EPSRC, NSF, ERC, Simons, Renaissance Philanthropy/XTX)

### Takeaway
Simons and XTX are the active funders, and both are backing AI-plus-maths capacity, not restrictions. Simons announced a $15M programme with OpenAI and Anthropic (15 Oct, just before the window; the institutes deadline of 1 Dec falls after it). XTX funded a £5M AI-assisted maths lab at Imperial (4 Nov). I found nothing new from UKRI/EPSRC, NSF or ERC.

### Cited Findings
- [confirmed] 15 Oct 2026 (just before the window): the Simons Foundation launched a $15M effort, "combined funding and resources", with Anthropic and OpenAI. It has three parts: an open call for workshops and initiatives on assessing AI-assisted results, adopting tools and developing shared standards; Targeted Grants to Math+AI Institutes; and AI tools and compute for selected Simons-supported researchers. President David Spergel: "The community should be driving discussions of how these tools are evaluated, adopted and used." — [Simons Foundation](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/news/simons-foundation-launches-15m-effort-to-help-mathematicians-navigate-ai-era/)
- [confirmed] The Math+AI Institutes call is aimed at established institutes or centres with physical space, not centres serving a single university. Deadline 1 Dec 2026, notification 31 Dec 2026, earliest start 1 Mar 2027. — [Simons RFA](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/funding/targeted-grants-to-mathai-institutes-2027/)
- [reported] 4 Nov 2026: Imperial College London announced a £5M grant (XTX Markets) to set up a "Langlands Lab" for AI-assisted mathematics, linked to Kevin Buzzard, with a focus on Langlands and Lean-verified results. The Imperial page returned 403, so this comes from the aggregator. — [Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/articles/admin-services/giving/2026/5-million-grant-to-establish-new-laboratory-for-ai-assisted-mathematics-at-imperial/), via [Proofs and Prompts newsfeed](https://proofsandprompts.com/newsfeed/)
- [reported] The Renaissance Philanthropy/XTX AI for Math Fund's 2026 round closed on 30 Mar 2026. No new round was found in the window. — [Renaissance Philanthropy](https://www.renaissancephilanthropy.org/ai-for-math-fund)
- [reported] 17 Nov 2026 (Guardian, 13 Nov): an ERC-funded project led by Jan von Plato is using Claude to speed up the deciphering of Gödel's notebooks. This is a use of AI in an ERC project, not an ERC policy. — [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths)
- [confirmed] Earlier context: in Sep 2026 the Simons Foundation and two other philanthropies made multiyear commitments to arXiv as it became an independent nonprofit. — [arXiv blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/)

### Inferences
- Private and philanthropic money (Simons, XTX), partly co-funded by the AI labs, is building the "AI-for-maths" infrastructure, while public funders stay quiet. That raises the independence concerns the AHM and Leiden Declaration have voiced, since Simons' partners are OpenAI and Anthropic.

### Gaps
- I found no window announcements from UKRI/EPSRC, NSF or ERC. My searches were not exhaustive on those agencies' news pages.
- The Imperial lab details are unverified (403).

## Q4. Updates from the AHM, the Fields Medallists' group and the Leiden Declaration

### Takeaway
The AHM was the most active group in the window. It passed 1,000 members and issued doctoral advising guidelines (3 Nov). I found no new output from the Fields Medallists' group or the Leiden Declaration organisers beyond the Fields Institute panel series ending on 17 Nov.

### Cited Findings
- [reported] 20 Oct 2026: the AHM has 1,008 members (graduate level and above), all committed to not working for commercial AI companies. 654 (about 65%) also signed an optional pledge not to use AI models in research. These are self-reported figures from a self-selected group. — [Magica](https://magica.com/news/human-mathematics-association-passes-1000-members-ai-commitments); also [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2593621-a-thousand-human-mathematicians-pledge-to-shun-ai-in-their-research/)
- [confirmed] 3 Nov 2026: the AHM communications group published "Doctoral Advising Guidelines" as a guest post on Tao's blog. The guidelines centre on human-authored writing, limited reliance on AI, ethical concerns and student wellbeing. According to commenters (not verified against the document), they include keeping pathways open for students who "wish to completely abstain from generative AI" and an "AI-free caucus". Full text at ahmath.org/doctoraladvising. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/03/doctoral-advising-guidelines-by-the-ahm/)
- [confirmed] The AHM statements page lists no new statement after the 7 Oct statement on OpenAI ("not a demonstration of scholarship, but a demonstration of power"). — [AHM statements](https://www.ahmath.org/statements)
- [confirmed] The Leiden Declaration news page has no items in the window. — [Leiden Declaration](https://leidendeclaration.ai/news)
- [reported] 19 Nov 2026: a "MisAnthropic Statement", reportedly signed by many prominent mathematicians and timed to Anthropic's IPO, accuses Anthropic of using safety branding to court investors. I did not verify the signatories. — [misanthropicstatement.com](https://misanthropicstatement.com) (via the newsfeed)

### Inferences
- The sceptical camp is moving from protest to building institutions: membership drives, advising guidelines, AI-free caucuses. It is also turning from OpenAI to the AI labs in general (the MisAnthropic statement).

### Gaps

## Q5. Notable essays, blog posts and talks by prominent mathematicians

### Takeaway
Tao's blog became the main community forum. He opened it to guest posts: Calegari's "Mathematics has changed. Let's act like it", the AHM guidelines, early-career surveys, and the MathHelm launch. The other main voices were Buzzard (what Lean verification does and does not establish), Aaronson (blogged on an AI-claimed proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture), and Gowers (a talk). The tone across these is mostly adaptive and pragmatic rather than doom-laden.

### Cited Findings
**Terence Tao / Tao's blog**
- [confirmed] 1 Nov: Frank Calegari (guest), "Mathematics has changed. Let's act like it". Six suggestions: (1) "Evaluate all work as if it is AI-assisted"; (2) hire on in-person interaction, not the paper file; (3) hold journals to higher standards of writing and exposition, and drop those that don't adapt; (4) broaden graduate education while keeping technical training; (5) refocus seminars on context and big questions; (6) take collective ownership of AI-produced results as shared heritage. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/)
- [confirmed] 2 Nov: Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue (guest), "Egoless Mathematics in the AI Era". They propose seeing mathematicians as bundles of productive abilities. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/02/egoless-mathematics-in-the-ai-era/)
- [confirmed] 4 Nov: Tao, "More of my interviews and talks". He says he is opening the blog to a wider range of voices on maths and AI. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/04/more-of-my-interviews-and-talks/)
- [confirmed] 5 Nov: "Futures of Maths" launched (Fournier-Facio, Kyrychenko, Roozenbeek, Wykowski), a platform surveying research mathematicians on AI's impact. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/)
- [confirmed] 6 Nov: Tao, "More posts". It links essays by Pavel Etingof ("Use of AI in Mathematical Research: A Guide for Young Mathematicians"), Anna Gilbert ("Math qua math", "Mathematical criticism"), Larry Guth ("What math means to me") and Atoosa Kasirzadeh ("A New Dawn For Mathematical Progress", Noema). — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/06/more-posts/)
- [confirmed] 8 Nov: Jana Gevertz and Steffen Marcus (guest), "What should we teach our (undergraduate) students?". They argue strong undergraduate programmes are needed to keep the ability to assess AI work. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/)
- [confirmed] 9 Nov: Brandenberger, Mularczyk and Woodruff (MIT, guest), "Our Survey of Early Career Mathematicians". An informal survey whose main concerns are job security and funding. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/)

- [confirmed] 19 Oct: Tao, "A sketch of the proof of non-existence of Siegel zeroes", a human digest of an AI-generated result. — [Tao blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/19/a-sketch-of-the-proof-of-non-existence-of-siegel-zeroes/)
- [reported] 19 Nov: Tao on Conversations with Tyler (recorded 11 Nov). — [CWT](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/terence-tao/)
- [reported] Earlier context: on 16 Oct the NYT reported that Tao called the coming results "toxic waste" beforehand and "maybe treated waste" after release. — [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/16/us/math-ai-terence-tao.html)

**Kevin Buzzard**
- [confirmed] 12 Nov: Xena blog post "…and the proof is checked in Lean". Of the 722 OpenAI papers, about 290 (around 45%) came with Lean code, and about 38 Lean packages do not state or prove the paper's main theorem (e.g. Lech's conjecture, where the code proves Corollary 5.4 instead). He warns that Lean compiling "does not mean that any corresponding pdf also contains a correct proof!" and that "one has to do some work beyond 'the Lean code compiles.'" He recommends `lake comparator` and multi-kernel checks. The Navier–Stokes formalisation (about 640K lines) compiles and is accepted by several kernels. — [Xena](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/11/12/and-the-proof-is-checked-in-lean/)

### Inferences
- The conversation is shifting from whether it is legitimate to how to absorb the results: reading groups, humanisation repositories (Hairer, Armstrong), forums (MathHelm), verification hygiene (Buzzard), and changes to hiring, teaching and refereeing (Calegari). This fits the session's optimistic framing, while critical voices (AHM, Kra, AGMAI) still press on process and access.
- Buzzard's numbers are a concrete corrective to "Lean-verified" headlines: fewer than half the papers came with Lean code, and some of that code proves weaker statements.
