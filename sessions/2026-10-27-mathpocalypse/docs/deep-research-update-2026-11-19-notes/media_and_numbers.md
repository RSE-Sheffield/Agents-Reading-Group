## 1. Significant coverage in the window: headline, date, angle, key quotes

### Takeaway
Within the window, the story moved from the Navier–Stokes and bulk-release backlash to a **second wave of OpenAI claims on 26 Oct 2026**: a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), local smoothing/Kakeya in all dimensions, and existence of 4D Yang–Mills. Anthropic posted a rival ETH refutation the same day. The coverage after that focused on verification. A KCL/Cambridge preprint found mismatches between the Navier–Stokes Lean code and the written proof. A Gross-led "shepherd" committee affirmed the Yang–Mills existence result on 9–10 Nov, which drew criticism over secrecy and scope. Scientific American was the dominant mainstream outlet. NPR's *Short Wave* covered the "is it hurting the field?" angle on 4 Nov.

## 2. Reported figures: cost, compute, attempted vs solved vs failed, error rates

### Takeaway
No in-window source reports a **dollar cost or GPU-hour figure** from OpenAI or Anthropic for any of these results. The figures that exist are lab-supplied: ~4,000 problems posed, 722 manuscripts in 372 families, an average of "~3 hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking" per result, and for Navier–Stokes ~10,000 agents, 88 h and 130 bn tokens. Counts after withdrawals and Lean-coverage percentages conflict across sources. For the 26 Oct results, Scientific American states that the prompts, compute time and chains of thought have not been made public.

### Cited Findings

**openai/math bulk release (6 Oct)**
- ~4,000 problems were posed, giving 722 manuscripts in 372 result families. **Reported** (OpenAI's own figure, relayed by press; I did not read the README directly) — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/openai-used-just-3-hours-of-chatgpt-pro-thinking-per-result-for-its-300-math-proofs-company-reveals/); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openai-drops-722-ai-math-171537220.html)
- "The average result used the equivalent compute of roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking." **Reported** (OpenAI README as quoted by OfficeChai; not independently verified).
  - The figure covers successful results only. Compute spent on the ~3,600+ problems that produced nothing is not disclosed.
  - Two results, the zeta zero-free region and Hodge for CM abelian varieties, were produced differently, with undisclosed compute.
  - — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/openai-used-just-3-hours-of-chatgpt-pro-thinking-per-result-for-its-300-math-proofs-company-reveals/); [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-largest-math-release-lean-proofs)
- Attempted vs failed: OpenAI has not published the full statistics. One secondary source says OpenAI is publishing statistics on attempted problems, but I did not find them. **Reported / gap** — [kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/openai-math-722-manuscripts-results-proofs-compute-costs/)
- A separate earlier OpenAI effort tested ~8,000 problems with a ~5% success rate and ~3 hours of GPT-Pro-level compute per problem. **Reported** (The Decoder; no primary link found) — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/some-mathematicians-call-for-openai-boycott-after-ai-generated-proofs-flood-their-field/)
- **Manuscript count after withdrawals is disputed:**
  - 719 after three were withdrawn for a sign error — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openai-drops-722-ai-math-171537220.html)
  - 721 after a 27 Oct repo update, from a single source — [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/openai-722-math-manuscripts-unreleased-model-2026/)
  - Scientific American described the release as "~400" results, and the 26 Oct coverage says "372 results," which likely means families rather than manuscripts.
- **Lean coverage is disputed:**
  - 162 of 722 manuscripts have a machine-checked main result, per a repo formalisation catalogue. Another source says 235 of 372 families link to a Lean document — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openai-drops-722-ai-math-171537220.html)
  - 42% of proofs formalised — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-math-solutions-arent-meeting-the-fields-standards-yet/)
  - The quasi-Riemann survey had no compiled or kernel-checked Lean — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openai-drops-722-ai-math-171537220.html)
- **Errors:** "some have already been retracted after outside scientists found mistakes" — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-unleashes-hundreds-more-math-results-upon-a-field-already-in-shock/). A [PRE-WINDOW, Sept 2026] human audit of ten OpenAI results found "no surviving confirmed substantive mathematical error in a principal result," but said several dependencies were only partly checked — [arXiv 2608.14673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14673). I found no overall error rate across the 722.

**Navier–Stokes (8 Sep)**
- ~10,000 agents, ~88 hours, ~130 billion tokens, 166 pages. **Reported** (OpenAI-sourced figures relayed by Scientific American) — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openais-blockbuster-navier-stokes-proof-isnt-what-we-thought-it-was/)
- Costs "reportedly in the millions of dollars." **Reported, unsourced** third-party estimate; treat as speculative — [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/openai-used-just-3-hours-of-chatgpt-pro-thinking-per-result-for-its-300-math-proofs-company-reveals/)
- Noam Brown attributes less than 10% of the success to the agent swarm. **Reported** — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/mathematicians-say-openai-doesnt-understand-its-own-navier-stokes-proof/)
- Lean and prose versions mismatch in at least one place. **Confirmed** as a claim of the KCL/Cambridge preprint, but the preprint is not peer reviewed — [KCL](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/openais-navier-stokes-proof-called-into-question)

**26 Oct results (ETH, Kakeya/local smoothing, Yang–Mills)**
- Prompts, compute time and chain-of-thought outputs have not been made public. No cost or compute figures were given — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/dueling-proofs-from-openai-and-anthropic-astound-scientists/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-exponential-time-hypothesis-false-kakeya-yang-mills)
- The ETH disproof was found on 16 Oct and Lean-checked on 17 Oct. **Reported** (OpenAI's timeline) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-exponential-time-hypothesis-false-kakeya-yang-mills)
- OpenAI gave $5m to the Simons Foundation for workshops on AI-produced results. **Reported** — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-exponential-time-hypothesis-false-kakeya-yang-mills)
- The Yang–Mills explanatory document runs to ~1,147 pages (a commenter says 1,146; Gil Kalai says ~1,200). **Reported** (blog) — [Woit](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=15986)
- Anthropic's ETH result: I found no compute or cost figures in any source.

### Inferences
- The only compute metric OpenAI offers is per successful result, measured in "ChatGPT Pro thinking hours." That cannot be turned into a total bill or a cost per success, because failure compute is undisclosed.
- An implied success rate of ~9–18% can be computed: 372 families or 722 manuscripts out of ~4,000 problems. But OpenAI selected what to publish, so this is a "published fraction," not a success rate. It should not be presented as one.
- Lean-coverage numbers vary by about a factor of 2 depending on the unit (manuscripts vs families) and the definition ("main result checked" vs "links to a Lean file").

### Gaps
- No GPU-hours, dollar costs or energy figures from any lab for any result.
- No full attempted/failed breakdown for openai/math. I could not confirm OpenAI's promised attempt statistics.
- No independent error rate across the release.
- I did not read the primary openai/math README or OpenAI's blog posts directly in this session, so all OpenAI figures above are via press.

## 3. How the framing has shifted (hype, scepticism, labour market, education)

### Takeaway
Framing went from awe at the Navier–Stokes claim in September, to anger at scale and norms after the 6 Oct bulk release (boycott calls, "demonstration of power"), to a lab race plus verification politics in the window. Coverage in the window is dominated by who verifies, how, and how openly. Labour-market and education angles are largely **absent** from the coverage I could reach.

### Inferences
- Scepticism has shifted from "are the proofs right?" to "is the process legitimate and legible?" Several results now carry Lean checks, but critics point to selection, secrecy, OpenAI-chosen reviewers, and proofs that nobody yet understands.
- For the session's light-touch, optimistic stance, two strands make good balancing material. Kalai's position and the Nature "field will survive" column are the optimistic strand. Woit and the AHM are the sharpest critics.
