# Open and crowdsourced AI-assisted mathematics, 19 Oct – 19 Nov 2026

Method note: the GitHub figures come from the GitHub API via `gh`, queried on 19 Nov 2026. erdosproblems.com pages were fetched directly. Tao's blog was read from the front page and the post pages. Labels: **confirmed** means seen in a primary source (repo, official page, the author's own post). **Reported** means secondary or self-published only. **Disputed** means contested in the sources.

## Formal Conjectures (google-deepmind/formal-conjectures): growth, resolved and formalised conjectures, notable PRs

### Takeaway
The repo is very busy. About 360 PRs were merged in the window and about 842 are still open. Most of the work adds Erdős and Green problems, links formal proofs through the new Palomar registry (made mandatory on 29 Oct), and fixes misformalisations, some of which an "AI audit tracker" flagged. Only a few conjectures were actually newly settled in the repo. The clearest are a Lean disproof of Kelly's conjecture (Erdős #338 variant) and WOWII Conjecture 19 being marked solved.

### Cited Findings
- **Volume (confirmed, 19 Nov 2026):** 360 PRs were merged between 19 Oct and 19 Nov 2026 (`gh pr list --search "merged:2026-10-19..2026-11-19"`). 842 PRs are open, and the repo has 1,365 stars and 624 forks. I have no baseline for these at the start of the window. — [repo](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures) / [merged PRs](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pulls?q=is%3Apr+is%3Amerged+merged%3A2026-10-19..2026-11-19)
- **Palomar linter, 29 Oct 2026 (confirmed):** PR #7631 by Paul-Lez adds `linter.style.formal_proof_palomar`. From now on, formal proofs of "research solved" declarations must link a [Palomar registry](https://palomar-registry.org/) entry. It grandfathers in "1,096 existing non-Palomar proof annotations on 1,070 declarations across 734 problem files". That is a useful measure of how many formal proofs are already linked. — [PR #7631](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7631)
- **Kelly's conjecture disproved in Lean, 8 Nov 2026 (confirmed as merged; the mathematical novelty is unclear):** PR #7788 by `veljjanoski` proves `erdos_338.variants.kelly_conjecture`, which says some basis of order 2 has no restricted order 3. It uses an explicit base-3 digit construction. The authors write that "the counterexample is our own. We could not access [He05], so it may differ from Hennecart's construction". This suggests the variant may already have been settled in the literature (Hennecart 2005). The PR excerpt does not say whether AI was used. A follow-up on 15 Nov (#7779) adds a positive-lower-density variant "with its Palomar proof". The same user also has a proof claim on the erdosproblems.com #338 thread. — [PR #7788](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7788), [PR #7779](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7779)
- **WOWII Conjecture 19 marked solved, 16 Nov 2026 (confirmed):** PR #7571 by `hamsade-google` (Graffiti/"Written on the Wall II") links an external Lean 4 proof of about 450 lines through Palomar entry PALOMAR-2026-11-16-000014. — [PR #7571](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7571)
- **WOWII Conjecture 291 refuted in Lean, 13 Nov 2026 (confirmed as merged):** PR #7248. — [PR #7248](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7248)
- **R(5,5) ≥ 43 formal proof linked, 14 Nov 2026 (confirmed):** PR #7998 by `Arth-Singh` uses `decide +kernel` on Exoo's 42-vertex colouring. This is a known result (Exoo 1989), now kernel-checked. — [PR #7998](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7998)

### Inferences

- Most contributors are individuals using GitHub handles, which fits the "amateurs or small teams" theme. Most PRs do not say whether AI was used.

### Gaps

- I don't know what Palomar is: who runs it or when it launched. It is referenced but I did not research it.

## Erdős problems: problems newly solved or disproved with AI help, counts, credit controversies

### Takeaway
The big story in this window is governance, not new solutions. On 6 Oct 2026, just before the window, Thomas Bloom froze problem comments and proof claims on erdosproblems.com, removed problem statuses and solved counts, and dropped credit language. The trigger was a flood of unexplained AI proof claims. Two days later he catalogued the OpenAI 6 Oct release, which makes claims on 25 Erdős problems. In the community database (teorth/erdosproblems), the solved counts did not change during the window: 579 solved, 642 open. The number of Lean-formalised problems rose from about 874 to 1,018.

### Cited Findings
- **Site changes, 6 Oct 2026 (confirmed; before the window but essential context):** Bloom's post "Changes" announces "a hiatus on problem comments and proof claims". The site "will not display the statuses of any problems", "the count of currently solved problems and the solved percentage will no longer be shown", and it will use "no credit/ownership language to describe future solutions". Links to Lean formalisations will be added "if verified on Palomar". His reason: "The main way that people publicly interact with the site now is to advertise their AI-generated proofs, often without any attempt to explain them, but as a way to record a (increasingly meaningless) priority claim." Site statistics in the post: 1,221 problems, more than 9,000 comments, almost 2,000 registered users, and 10,000–25,000 unique visitors a day. — [erdosproblems.com blog: Changes](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/blog:9)
- **OpenAI release catalogued, 8 Oct 2026 (confirmed as Bloom's summary; the claims themselves are unverified):** OpenAI's 6 Oct GitHub release contained "372 significant proof claims". 25 of them concern Erdős problems: 3, 90, 120, 138, 148, 172, 174, 181, 184, 293, 304, 371, 416, 431, 508, 551, 604, 802, 821, 952, 968, 970, 986, 1083 and 1150. Twelve of these are on the FrontierMath Erdős list. "21 out of the 25 proofs have been formalised in Lean." The PDFs total 1,347 pages. The headline claim is quasipolynomial Szemerédi bounds for all k≥3 (Erdős #3, a 198-page PDF), but Bloom notes that the Lean code seems to prove only a weaker bound, exp(−(log log N)^{1+c_k})N. He mentions "accusations of plagiarism" and that some proofs had appeared online before. He calls the papers "very poorly written, in the usual AI fashion" but likely "correct". Comments were still being posted in the window, for example on 25 Oct. — [erdosproblems.com blog: Erdős problems in the OpenAI proofs](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/blog:10)
- **Expositions in the window (confirmed, limited):** the latest "Recent Expositions" on the homepage are for problems 293, 148 and 304, all dated 14 Oct 2026, and all three are on the OpenAI list. Nothing newer was posted up to 19 Nov. — [erdosproblems.com](https://www.erdosproblems.com/)
- **Forum proof claims stopped (confirmed):** every entry in the forum's "Proof claims" list is "a month ago", consistent with the freeze. Recent claimants include veljjanoski (#338), BorisAlexeev (#701), aimir (#1162) and jbecart (#1110). — [erdosproblems.com forum](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/)
- **Database counts (confirmed):** the statistics history for the community GitHub database shows no change in status from 18 Sep to 12 Nov 2026: 1,221 problems, 579 solved (336 proved, 139 disproved, 104 "solved"), 642 open. Lean-formalised problems rose from 874 (19 Oct) to 1,018 (12 Nov), and problems with a Lean-solved statement from 303 (19 Sep) to 318. The last human commits were on 17 Oct (Tao, "record the $100 prize on problem 552"). Every commit in the window is the bot syncing formalisation status from formal-conjectures. — [statistics_history.csv](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/blob/main/data/statistics_history.csv), [commits](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/commits/main)
- **Tao/community revision repo for the OpenAI write-ups (reported):** `teorth/oai-math-revised` was created on 18 Oct 2026 and pushed on 27 Oct, with 15 commits and 2 stars. The repo's tagline is "Math is of the people, by the people, and for the people, and we will cite the people". It invites PRs to "augment lit reviews/cite related results", "improve exposition" and fix errors in OpenAI's write-ups. The README links to `mathcommunity/oai-math-revised`, so it may be a fork, and who leads it is unclear. The README quotes OpenAI's catalogue as "719 manuscripts organized into 372 families". — [teorth/oai-math-revised](https://github.com/teorth/oai-math-revised)
- **Manuscript count discrepancy (disputed):** OpenAI's README, as quoted in that repo, says 719 manuscripts. The MathHelm guest post on Tao's blog (13 Nov) says 722. Bloom counts 372 proof claims, which matches the 372 families. — [oai-math-revised](https://github.com/teorth/oai-math-revised); [Tao's blog, MathHelm](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/13/)
- **Amateur example from before the window (reported):** a recent secondary article, dated about 8 Nov, revisits forum user Liam Price's use of GPT-5.4 Pro on Erdős #1196, an April/May 2026 result with a 35-page preprint on 1 May. Its counterexample to #1202 (Price/GPT-5.4 Pro) was formalised by Codex and linked in formal-conjectures on 27 Oct. — [ecoticias](https://www.ecoticias.com/en/a-forum-user-asked-an-ai-model-about-an-erdos-problem-that-had-been-open-for-60-years-and-the-trick-hidden-in-its-proof-is-now-settling-other-puzzles-mathematicians-had-left-unsolved/53752/); [formal-conjectures PR #7638](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/7638)

### Inferences
- Bloom's freeze means the site no longer gives a "solved count" signal, so any AI-solved count after 6 Oct has to come from other sources such as formal-conjectures or Palomar, not erdosproblems.com. This is a notable point for the discussion: the main showcase for crowdsourced AI maths deliberately stepped back from scorekeeping.
- The credit dispute has moved from individual forum priority claims to the OpenAI bulk release: plagiarism accusations, the "cite the people" repo and MathHelm.

### Gaps
- I found no specific Erdős problem newly solved or disproved with AI help *within* 19 Oct – 19 Nov. The site no longer records this, and the GitHub database status did not change. The Kelly variant of #338 (formal-conjectures, 8 Nov) is the nearest candidate, and AI involvement there is not stated.
- I did not access Tao's "AI contributions to Erdős problems" wiki page for updates in the window.
- I did not read the plagiarism accusations about the OpenAI release in detail.

## Mathlib milestones and Lean FRO announcements

### Takeaway
Steady infrastructure progress. Lean v4.36.0 shipped on 16 Nov and v4.37.0-rc1 on 18 Nov, and Mathlib took about 809 commits in the window. The mathlib3 repo was archived, a Lean-in-Lean verified kernel was announced just before the window, and Tau Ceti, an AI-authored Mathlib-based library incubated by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, announced an Anthropic grant on 30 Oct.

### Cited Findings
- **Lean releases (confirmed):** v4.36.0-rc2 on 27 Oct, v4.36.0 on 16 Nov and v4.37.0-rc1 on 18 Nov 2026. v4.35.0 came out on 12 Oct. — [lean4 releases](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/releases)
- **Mathlib activity (confirmed):** 809 commits to leanprover-community/mathlib4 since 19 Oct 2026 (GitHub API count, 19 Nov). — [mathlib4 commits](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/commits/master)
- **mathlib3 archived (confirmed):** the API shows `archived=true` and a last push on 6 Nov 2026. The secondary report gives 6 Nov as the archive date. — [mathlib3](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib3)
- **Verified kernel, 15 Oct 2026 (reported, self-published; just before the window):** Leonardo de Moura's blog announces "con-leche", described as the first Lean kernel written in Lean and proven consistent, which checks the full Mathlib export in about 100 seconds on 8 threads. — [de Moura blog](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-10-15-lean-now-ships-with-verified-kernels/)
- **Tau Ceti (confirmed as the project's own page):** "Open, AI-authored, scalable mathematics library in Lean, built on Mathlib." Agents review PRs against "adversarial rubrics, hunting mis-formalizations and vacuous statements". Its latest news is "An Anthropic grant for Tau Ceti · 30 Oct 2026". Release v4.36.0-rc2 was tagged on 3 Nov 2026. It is "incubated by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative". — [taucetiproject.org](https://taucetiproject.org/)
- **FRO six-month roadmaps from Sept 2026 (reported, secondary only).** — [invarians wiki](https://invarians.org/wiki/Lean_theorem_prover/History/Lean_since_2023)

### Inferences
- The Lean ecosystem is adapting to agent-generated proofs: the verified kernel, Tau Ceti's agent review, and Palomar linking in formal-conjectures.

### Gaps
- I found no official Lean FRO blog post or Mathlib "milestone" announcement, such as a theorem-count landmark, dated within the window. I did not search Lean Zulip.
- The terms of the Tau Ceti grant are not detailed.

The optimisation-constants repo was heavily curated on 26–28 Oct. Tao's blog in the window was dominated by guest essays on AI and maths, plus "Lean Grimoire" and MathHelm.

### Cited Findings
- **ETP end-to-end proof, 26 Oct 2026 (confirmed):** PR #1506 by `vlad902` is "an end-to-end proof certifying that the ETP has correctly resolved all implications between all 4,694 equations", by checking a pre-processed certificate. It adds under 6 minutes of build time. leanchecker was re-enabled in CI on the same day (#1507). — [PR #1506](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories/pull/1506)
- **ETP paper under review (confirmed):** referee-response commits on 1 Nov (#1512, #1514, #1519, #1520) and "Paper updates" on 18 Nov (#1528). — [equational_theories commits](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories/commits/main)
- **Optimisation constants (confirmed):** at least 100 commits since 19 Oct, mostly merges on 26–28 Oct. Entries include C_5a recording the Carter–O'Bryant–Tao bound 0.94304 and Hou–Zhao's 0.9435, plus "three forum claims from Erdős Problem #30 thread" (28 Oct). They also include C_28, a dimension-9 Borsuk bound, and "asterisks on both 2026 Linnik bounds" (PR #205). - **Tao's blog in the window (confirmed):** posts include 1 Nov "Mathematics has changed.
